Consolidated from the blueprint's risk register and the architectural decisions it flagged as expensive to reverse.

Technical risks

Scheduling concurrency

The hardest correctness problem in the product, made harder by MySQL's lack of exclusion constraints. A double-booking in a demo loses the deal.

Odontogram complexity

Consistently underestimated. Surfaces, supersession, mixed dentition, bridges spanning teeth, RTL rendering. Prototype early with real dentists.

Retrofitting immutability

If Phase 4 ships with mutable chart entries "to save time", the clinical history is permanently lost. Not recoverable by a later migration.

The existing codebase

No tests, no CI, unlogged failures, live security holes. Building clinical features on this substrate without Phase 0 compounds risk with every commit.

Image storage economics

Radiographs are large and must be retained for years. Model storage cost per patient per year before pricing the product, not after.

Business risks

Scope collapse

The largest risk in the plan. An MVP that grows to include insurance, portal, lab and AI will never ship. Defend the Phase 0–6 boundary ruthlessly.

Migration friction

Practices do not switch because switching means moving years of records. Data import is not a feature, it is a sales requirement.

Clinical validation

Building dental software without a practising dentist embedded produces a product that looks right and works wrong. Recruit clinical advisors before Phase 4.

Regulatory surprise

Data residency and health-data rules vary by market and change. Discovering a residency requirement after signing an enterprise customer is an expensive re-architecture.

Support burden

Clinical software generates support load orders of magnitude beyond a supply app. A clinic that cannot book patients calls immediately.

A breach ends the company

Patient data breaches in health software are existential, not recoverable. The Phase 0 security posture is a business decision, not a technical one.

Competing on features

You will not out-feature twenty-year-old products. Win on language, channel, onboarding speed and procurement integration.

Abandoning the marketplace

The supplier marketplace is the only advantage competitors cannot replicate. Phase 8's procurement bridge is where that moat gets built.

Assumptions carried by this roadmap

  • No calendar durations or dates were proposed in the source roadmap; effort is expressed as complexity only. Any scheduling must be added by the delivery team.
  • All phases begin at Not Started. No work is recorded as complete.
  • The supplier marketplace remains in the product; Phase 8 assumes it exists to receive purchase requests.
  • PostgreSQL is recommended but the decision is an explicit Phase 0 task, not a settled fact.
  • Insurance claims, DICOM viewing, patient portal, offline mode and all AI are deliberately outside the MVP.
